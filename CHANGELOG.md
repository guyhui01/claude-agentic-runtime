- **Dispatch parameter manifests, second of ten: WF-002 "Delivery Agile SAFe".** Eight identity-card parameters derived from the card's `ART CONTEXT` block, pinned to catalog `v4.2.0`, and registered in `DEFAULT_MANIFESTS` — registration being what makes a manifest take effect, since an unregistered route reports an honest `paramsChecked: false` rather than failing. - **Third of ten: WF-003 "AI Application Launch".** Eight `TECHNICAL CONTEXT` parameters, seven of them carrying an explicit enumeration on the card, so the detectors are the card's own families rather than a judgement about prose. Nothing here declares `defaultValue`: this card offers neither a sanctioned unknown nor an operator-profile constant, so every parameter is required. Checked against the dry-run oracle before its tests were written — the practice introduced with WF-002 — and it diverges in two places, both resolved at the card and both documented on the specification concerned. `Cloud provider` is stated in the brief's constraint list ("existing Azure tenancy"), which is why the technical detectors read `constraints` alongside the prose — the table lists it must-ask because it was computed on a thinner input, not because it missed the fact. `Target LLM` was classed as an operator-profile default, which conflates the model the **client's application** will run on — a sovereignty and cost decision the card lists "to fill in before starting" — with the model a live run of this runtime is routed to; it stays required. The result carries the same count the oracle predicted with a different composition. Its `GDPR constraints` line turned out to be the first case of a class, and produced the rule below.
- **Fourth of ten: WF-004 "AI Consulting Engagement".** Eight `ENGAGEMENT CONTEXT` lines become **ten specifications** — `Client [Name / Sector / Size]` is a conjunction and is split, so an operator who has already named the client is asked for the size, not for "Client". The hardest card so far, and not for its length: WF-002 and WF-003 describe technical objects whose vocabulary is unmistakable (an ART, a vector store), while this one describes a **commercial situation** in words any brief may use — a client, a duration, stakeholders, stakes. The exposure is therefore the false "filled", and two detectors were tightened against it before any test was written: `Engagement scope` no longer accepts a bare "support" ("decision support", "we support the team" are ordinary prose, not the card's Support engagement), and `Client AI maturity` no longer accepts a bare "advanced" (an "advanced RAG pipeline" says nothing about where the *client* stands; the word only counts beside the maturity vocabulary). Neither tightening is needed to make the fixture pass — both close a gap the neutral-prose probe cannot see, because these words are plausible domain vocabulary rather than absent vocabulary, and both are locked by a test that turns red when the detector is loosened back. `Expected deliverables` takes a detector rather than a `defaultValue`, unlike `Constraints`: intake guarantees the field is affirmative and non-empty, not that it names one of the card's four families. `submittedBy` is deliberately not mapped onto `Stakeholders` — filling a card parameter from the submitter's role would make the verdict depend on *who* submits the brief, the exact invariance the P19/P20 role probes proved live. The card's "— estimate" on maturity is not a sanctioned unknown: an estimate is a value. Against the dry-run checklist the divergences are the documented class — the qualified P04 fixture states the engagement window, the sponsor and two stakes that its sketch does not — and one prediction of our own was corrected by the check rather than the reverse: `Stakeholders` is answered by the constraint "executive readout expected", which names an audience.
- **Fifth of ten: WF-005 "Strategic Intelligence & Growth".** Seven `INTELLIGENCE CONTEXT` lines become seven specifications — the slash rule finds no conjunction here, and the one trap is typographic: in `[AI/LLM / AI consulting / …]` the slash of `AI/LLM` is internal to an item, not a separator. All seven are required, with no `defaultValue` and no sanctioned unknown, because each feeds a **backbone** step's input (STEP-01: scope, sources, horizon · STEP-02: audience · STEP-03: format, tone). And `Request type` refuses the bare token `competition` although the card lists it as a value, because the same word is the label of a **different line of the same card**; counting it bare would make two specifications non-independent, the WF-005 `LinkedIn` policy applied inside one card instead of across three lines. `Selection criteria` carries the weakest **necessity** claim of the fourteen, and it is recorded rather than smoothed: a search for "criteri" across the whole card returns exactly one hit, this parameter block itself, so no step names it as an input or an output; it stays required because the dry-run table classes it must-ask on **both** of this workflow's briefs, and it is the first candidate should one ever be demoted. `Decision-makers` applies a two-regime admission: **titles count bare** (a C-level or a named sponsor unambiguously answers who decides), **functions do not** — `procurement` names a department in "the procurement portal" as readily as a decision owner, so it counts only beside decision vocabulary. That is stricter than the shipped precedent, WF-004 `Stakeholders` admitting a bare `sponsor` and a bare `board` with no adjacency guard at all. The detectors were measured against the nineteen coverage-matrix briefs **before** any test existed, and that found one defect in the first draft: `Response deadline` accepted a deadline word beside a bare quantity and filled on the WF-004 fixture's "phase-one deadline in six weeks" — a delivery milestone inside an **already signed** engagement, which is the opposite of a bid response. That is the anchorless quantity WF-005 `Horizon` refused and the cross-vocabulary probe had to remove from WF-004 `Engagement duration`; letting a third manifest disagree with them would have been the same policy applied in two directions for the third time. The card's "RFP deadline — ISO 8601" annotation is a deliberate divergence: the ISO form is accepted but not required, since enforcing it would reject this workflow's own fixture ("the response is due in three weeks") — a format hint is a data-entry preference, not the card's contract.

- **A policy-consistency audit across every registered manifest, frozen as a reviewed table — built at six manifests rather than at ten, and deliberately.** Every other guard here judges ONE manifest against ITS OWN fixture, so a question of policy settled in opposite directions across two of them is invisible: both are green and each is locally right. That defect has occurred twice and was attempted a third time — "a bare quantity is not a value", refused by WF-005 `Horizon`, admitted by WF-004 `Engagement duration`, re-attempted by WF-006 `Response deadline`. The cross-vocabulary probe catches these only by luck, needing a foreign brief that happens to expose the divergence; this one reads the manifests themselves and puts the siblings side by side. It renders three tables — which brief fields each spec reads, which policy markers each carries (negation guard, quantity vocabulary, adjacency window, sanctioned unknown, declared default), and, for each fact carried by several cards, the vocabulary unique to each copy. Like the cross-vocabulary matrix it is **not** an assertion of correctness: cards genuinely differ, so a divergence is not mechanically a defect, and the value is in reading the diff. One hard assertion accompanies it, because it is an invariant rather than a judgement: `submittedBy` is mapped by no manifest — now a **measured** fact, obtained by calling each mapping with a distinct sentinel per field, rather than a sentence repeated in six comments. A second, "every required spec has a detector or a declared default", was drafted and then removed: it rebuilds machinery weighed and cut on 2026-07-28 as redundant, and the redundancy was re-falsified rather than taken on trust — deleting a required spec's `pattern` turns five existing tests red, the manifest's own happy-path among them. The table also records what it does **not** measure: it pairs specs by a name convention, so a divergence it cannot pair is a divergence it cannot see; its adjacency marker reads `{0,N}` only, so a quantity anchored by an immediately following unit noun shows no marker and the row is a prompt to read rather than a verdict; and it reads regex sources, not behaviour, which is the cross-vocabulary probe's job — neither subsumes the other. Timing argument, taken from the cross-vocabulary probe's own entry: at the end this would be a machine for *finding* divergences, meaning retrofits across ten files; here it is a machine for *preventing* them, since the four remaining manifests are written against a visible table.

Added, and this one is **not** a no-op — it fills on three foreign briefs, moved the cross-vocabulary matrix from 35 cells to 38, and turned a shipped WF-004 test red, which is the guard working rather than a regression; the expectation was updated with its reason. And WF-006 `Prospect (AI maturity)` carried a comment claiming the same tightening as WF-004 while being materially wider — a legitimate divergence, since WF-004's card offers an enumeration to key on and this fact sits inside a conjunction with no list at all, but one that had to be written down rather than left to read as an accident. **The audit itself had to be made trustworthy before its output was acted on, and it took four corrections.** Its first two renderings raised false divergences by splitting alternations inside groups and by gluing the `b` of `\b` onto the literal following it, reporting one word as two divergences — a table that cries wolf gets ignored, which is worse than no table. Then its marker for the quantity family — the one policy question this audit exists for — was measured against a written definition rather than eyeballed, and found **25% sensitive**: it reported three specs where twelve carry quantity vocabulary, an authoritative-looking table with nine of twelve members invisible. Widening it to a bare `\d` swung it the other way to fifteen, pulling in a date literal and a model name, which would have been the harder error to notice. The definition is now fixed in the file and the implementation is checked against it, not against whichever count looked plausible. With the marker correct, the first full reading has a result worth stating: **no bare-quantity spec survives in any of the six manifests** — the five rows showing quantity vocabulary without an adjacency window all anchor the number to an adjacent literal or unit noun (`\d+\s+(developers|teams)`, `PI[-\s]?\d+`, `\d+\s*(employees|staff)`), verified spec by spec.

- **A cross-vocabulary probe over every registered manifest, frozen as a reviewed snapshot.** Each manifest is run against every *foreign* coverage-matrix brief — one whose expected route is another workflow, the four honest NO_MATCH briefs included — and the filled cells are rendered as a matrix committed alongside the test. It is deliberately **not** an assertion of correctness and must not become one: a filled foreign cell is not automatically a defect, since some facts legitimately cross cards (a brief stating GDPR *has* stated a compliance stake). What it refuses is an **unnoticed** change, so the value is in reading the diff when the matrix moves, never in the red/green — a new manifest necessarily changes it, and a reflexive regeneration would turn the file into a rubber stamp. Run at five manifests rather than ten on purpose: waiting for the tenth would let five more be written under a policy that may have drifted. One guard-the-guard assertion keeps a filtered fixture set or a shrunken registry from producing an empty matrix that reads as "no leak". - **A discrimination probe is blind to a detector keying on vocabulary its neutral brief happens to lack.** Widening WF-005's `Intelligence scope` to a bare `AI` token left the entire suite green: the probe's prose carries no AI vocabulary, and every other case involving that specification expects it filled. Corrected in the same lot with a case on the AI-dense WF-001 brief, which states not one intelligence parameter and must leave all seven missing. Seven falsifications were run against this manifest and each turns a **different** test red.
- **Slash rule: a bracketed `/` list means "or" on some card lines and "and" on others, and the manifest must state which.** `AWS / GCP / Azure` is an enumeration — pick one. `Personal data: YES/NO — Data location: EU/US` is a conjunction — supply both; so are `Name / Sector / Size` on WF-004 and WF-007, `Prospect` on WF-006, three lines on WF-008, and one each on WF-009 and WF-010. Reading one as the other inverts the semantics in one of two bad directions — an enumeration read as a conjunction never passes, a conjunction read as an enumeration passes on one fact out of three — and there is no safe default to fall back on, so the call is made from the card's wording and stated in a comment on the line concerned. A conjunction is now **split into one specification per fact** under a qualified label (`GDPR constraints (personal data)`, `GDPR constraints (data location)`), so `PARAMS_MISSING` names the half that is actually absent instead of answering "GDPR constraints" to an operator who has already supplied the location and leaving them nothing to act on. Consequence for planning: a manifest can hold more specifications than its card holds lines — WF-003 ships nine for eight — so the 81-parameter count read off the cards is a floor, not a total.
- **The 2026-07-19 dry-run table (§2 of the identity-card dry run) is consulted before every manifest — as a checklist of the parameters that are hard to obtain, not as an acceptance oracle.** It was computed against the coverage-matrix **sketches** (the raw stakeholder asks), while a manifest check runs against the **qualified fixture briefs**, which are richer: P02's sketch names no PI duration, P03's names no cloud provider, P04's names neither the sponsor role nor the engagement window. Those are two different questions on two different inputs, so a divergence is a signal to settle the point at the card — never a verdict on either side. An earlier version of this entry read those divergences as table errors and blamed a skipped `constraints` array; that diagnosis was wrong and is corrected in the document's own annotation rather than edited out of it. What the comparison does earn is the other direction: it caught a genuine defect in the code, where the first `ART name` detector required the proper noun to *precede* ART — encoding the word order of the card's example rather than what the field means — and would have asked the operator for a name the brief already carried.
- **A discrimination probe across every registered manifest.** Given prose that is grammatical, plausible and carries none of any card's vocabulary, every required parameter must come back missing; one that survives is keying on something other than its card. It covers each new manifest the moment it is registered, with no per-manifest fixture to write, and it measures the one failure the fail-closed detector cannot see — a pattern that is present but too permissive. Verified by falsification: replacing one WF-002 detector with a permissive `/.+/` turns the probe red.

### 🔧 Fixed
- **Half the CI matrix was spent on a dead Node runtime, and the Active LTS was never exercised.** `build-test` ran Node **20** and **22**: Node 20 reached **end of life on 2026-03-24**, four months before this entry, while Node **24** — the Active LTS, and a version `engines: ">=20"` claimed to support — was tested by nothing. Matrix moved to **22 (Maintenance LTS) + 24 (Active LTS)**, and `engines` to `">=22"` in the same lot, because claiming support for a runtime nothing tests is the same class of empty guarantee as a guard that never runs. Node 26 (Current) is the workstation's own version and is covered by every local run; that 24 sits between a tested 22 and a daily-used 26 is an inference, and the first CI run on it is the proof. Both jobs also gain `timeout-minutes: 10` against an observed duration of 23-28 s, so a hung job becomes a fast red instead of hours of silence. Measured while re-examining that job: the new integration job adds a ~1 s shallow clone to the same steps and runs **in parallel** with the matrix, so it costs runner minutes — free on a public repository — and no wall-clock. It also needs no secret, so pull requests from forks still pass.
- **CI verified none of the assertions that need the real catalog.** The `build-test` job checks out this repository alone — deliberate, since ADR-0002 keeps the runtime independent of the catalog — so every `real-sidecar` block skipped there. Measured: **28 assertions were unverified by CI** (316 passed / 23 skipped locally against 299 / 40 without a catalog; the 17-test delta is smaller only because eleven trivial fallbacks pass in their place). Those 28 are every backbone resolved against real agent prose, the plan built verbatim from the real WF-001 card, the route validated against the real sidecar, and the five manifest catalog-tag comparisons. A new `catalog-integration` job closes it, and three of its four decisions are corrections of a first design rather than choices made once:
  - **It clones the catalog at the tag the manifests PIN, not at the default branch.** `main` was the first design and reading ADR-0002 overturned it: that ADR rejects a live import precisely because "a catalog edit would break the runtime with no safety net", and accepts as a stated cost that "freshness is not immediate — you must bump to propagate". A job on `main` turns a catalog *release* into a red build here until the manifests are re-derived — converting a deliberately unhurried freshness into an urgency, and leaving a standing red that hides real regressions. At the pinned tag the tag comparison is tautological only for the manifest whose pin was read; for the other four it still catches an internal disagreement (falsified: pinning `wf-002` to `v4.1.0` turns its assertion red). Drift against the *latest* catalog tag is therefore answered as a session-start fact in `next_steps.md` — a difference there is freshness debt, not a failure.
  - **The tag is read from the code, never written in the workflow**, which would be a second source of truth for the pin, free to drift from the manifests it mirrors.
  - **The catalog is cloned anonymously rather than checked out with an action.** `actions/checkout`'s own README states `${{ github.token }}` is scoped to the current repository and says nothing about cross-repository *public* reads; the claim that the default token suffices was an inference, not a reading. A shallow HTTPS clone of a public repo needs no token, so the question does not arise, and it fails loudly on a tag that does not exist. Verified by running the exact command.
  - **It runs `npm test`, not a `real-sidecar` filter**, because a substring filter is itself a failure mode: rename a file out of the pattern and the job runs fewer tests while staying green.
  - **`REQUIRE_CATALOG` is what makes the job's green mean something.** The first version asserted that the cloned `sidecar.json` existed — which proves the clone, not that the tests read it: a mistyped `CATALOG_ROOT` in the step below would have skipped every real-sidecar block and passed, reproducing the defect the job exists to close. The demand now travels with the run instead: when that variable is set, a `no-catalog fallback` block firing is a **failure** rather than a skip, and both variables are declared once at job level so they cannot disagree. Falsified in three states: the pinned catalog with the flag → 316 passed / 23 skipped; a mistyped path with the flag → **11 failures**; the same absence *without* the flag → green, so a contributor with no catalog checkout is unaffected. And against a catalog extracted at `v4.1.0`, seven tests fail including all five tag comparisons.
  - **No billed call can fire there**, verified on three legs rather than assumed: the twelve live harnesses require `LIVE_RUN` *in addition* to a catalog, the one other `*live*` file is hermetic by construction (mocked `query()`, interim sidecar, stub resolver), and the runner refuses `ANTHROPIC_API_KEY`.
  - **Stated rather than implied, since this job's red is a signal and not a barrier:** `main` carries no branch protection, so no status check is required and nothing is mechanically blocked. The last CI conclusion therefore joins the session-start factual check beside the open-alerts count and the freshness comparison. Remaining limits, named: the job triggers on push and pull request to `main`; and a card edited in the catalog without a re-tag leaves the sidecar's tag unchanged and passes.
